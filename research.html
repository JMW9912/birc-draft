Research

Co-Embodied Intelligence

We investigate how intelligence can move across bodies: from human sensorimotor experience to robotic embodiment, and back through physical and multimodal feedback.

Research at a Glance
Core Hypothesis

Shared Physical Intelligence

Different bodies may share common physical intelligence. We seek embodiment-invariant representations of motion, contact, force, object interaction, and intent that enable sensorimotor knowledge to transfer across humans and robots. The loop is closed through tactile, haptic, visual, and physical feedback, allowing humans and robots to co-adapt rather than rely on one-way teleoperation.

Research Questions

What We Are Trying to Understand

RQ 01 · Sense

How can we capture human sensorimotor intelligence?

We develop soft, wearable, and multimodal interfaces that capture whole-body motion, hand motion, contact, force, and physical state without constraining natural behavior.

Soft sensingWearablesHaptics
RQ 02 · Translate

How can intelligence transfer across different bodies?

We study cross-embodiment representations that preserve task-relevant motion and contact information even when human and robot morphologies, sensing spaces, and actuation spaces differ.

RetargetingRepresentation learningSkill transfer
RQ 03 · Embody

How can robots physically perceive and interact with the world?

We integrate tactile perception, compliant mechanisms, dexterous manipulation, whole-body control, and humanoid platforms to realize intelligence through physical interaction.

Tactile perceptionHumanoidsManipulation
RQ 04 · Co-adapt

How can humans and robots learn from each other?

We move beyond one-way demonstration toward bidirectional co-adaptation: robots learn from human intervention and sensorimotor experience, while humans receive meaningful feedback from robotic interaction.

Shared autonomyRobot learningEmbodied AI
